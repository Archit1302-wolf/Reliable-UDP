# Plot 4 & Plot 5 Paragraphs (Section 3 - Results)

### Plot 4: Retransmission Timeout Policy Comparison (5% Loss, Window = 32)
Plot 4 compares the achieved goodput across three RTO policies—adaptive (RFC 6298 with Karn's algorithm), adaptive-nokarn, and a static 500 ms fixed timer—for both Go-Back-N and Selective Repeat under a sustained 5% packet loss rate and window size $W=32$. For Selective Repeat, the adaptive policy attains a mean goodput of 2.77 Mbps compared to only 0.53 Mbps for `fixed:0.5`, demonstrating a 5.2x (423%) throughput advantage. Similarly, for Go-Back-N, adaptive achieves 1.26 Mbps versus 0.28 Mbps for fixed, delivering a 4.5x speedup. This sharp disparity arises because every lost packet under the fixed policy incurs an unyielding 500 ms idle stall—approximately ten times the underlying 50 ms path RTT. In contrast, disabling Karn’s algorithm (`adaptive-nokarn`) yields 2.71 Mbps for SR and 1.25 Mbps for GBN, performing within 2% of standard adaptive because retransmission events at 5% loss are sufficiently dispersed that uncorrected RTT samples do not drastically destabilize steady-state goodput.

### Plot 5: Adaptive RTO Estimation and Karn's Ambiguity Dynamics
Plot 5 details the temporal evolution of RTO estimates alongside empirical RTT samples for Selective Repeat ($W=32$, 5% loss, seed 1), contrasting Karn's algorithm enabled against disabled. Under the baseline adaptive estimator, the RTO trajectory closely tracks the measured RTT cloud (mean RTT of 48.9 ms), maintaining a tightly calibrated safety cushion with a mean RTO of 61.0 ms—exactly 12.1 ms above the average latency, which matches the expected $4 \times \text{RTTVAR}$ spread. Transient peaks exceeding 100 ms (peaking at 117.1 ms) stem entirely from exponential backoff during back-to-back losses, rapidly collapsing back to the baseline estimate upon window advancement via `forward()`. With Karn's rule active, 927 unambiguous samples are recorded, while disabling Karn captures 1024 samples (mean RTO 61.7 ms); the 97 filtered samples prevent inflated variance calculations from delayed retransmissions, guaranteeing long-term estimator integrity.
